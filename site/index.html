4. Formulation updates

What formulators can now use, must drop, or must relabel. Patent data was not searched in this pass.

RegulatoryUS

Bemotrizinol: formulation conditions in the FDA order

Allowed up to 6%. It may be combined with any permitted sunscreen active except aminobenzoic acid or trolamine salicylate. Sprays are allowed only if made and packaged without propellant, or in a system where the propellant never contacts the formulation. DSM, the requester, holds 18 months of exclusive marketing from 9 Aug 2026. The ingredient itself has been used in Europe and elsewhere for decades.

RegulatoryEU

Reformulation pipeline in the EU

Omnibus VIII is already applying (from 1 May 2026). The draft Omnibus IX would add benzophenone-1, benzophenone-2, two hair-dye colourants and prostaglandin-like substances to the banned list, with 12 to 24 month transition periods. Eyelash and brow serums using prostaglandin analogues are the most exposed product type.

8. Pharma and cosmetics: ingredients that cross over, and ones that cannot

The same molecule can be a drug in one setting and barred from cosmetics in another. Each case below separates the R&D reason from the regulatory reason. Items marked "background" come from general knowledge, not the linked sources.

Prostaglandin analogues (bimatoprost and relatives): eye drugs, not lash cosmetics
Technical and R&D

Bimatoprost is a prostaglandin analogue used in medicines to lower eye pressure, and its eyelash-growth effect led to a drug for eyelash hypotrichosis. Analogues then appeared in lash cosmetics. They are known to cause eye and surrounding-tissue adverse effects, and the SCCS noted they can act at very low concentrations near the eye.

Background: these are potent receptor-active molecules, so the growth effect is a pharmacological effect, not a cosmetic one.

Regulatory

The SCCS (2022) raised safety concerns, and its 2025 opinion found none of the assessed analogues safe for lash and brow growth. France's Anses warned in 2025. A German authority classified such a serum as a medicinal product by function. The EU draft would add prostaglandin-like substances to the banned list.

Injectables and "treatment" actives: outside the cosmetic definition in India
Technical and R&D

Cosmetic products are designed for external application. An injected product bypasses the skin barrier, which is a different exposure and risk profile from anything tested for topical cosmetics.

Background: injection also demands sterility and clinical evidence that cosmetic safety files do not cover.

Regulatory

CDSCO's 18 May 2026 notice says injectables are not cosmetics, cannot be used by consumers, professionals or aesthetic clinics under the cosmetic label, and that cosmetics cannot be promoted or used for treatment. Using prohibited ingredients is a violation of the Act and Rules.

SCC Online ChemRadar Freyr
Sunscreen filters: a cosmetic in many countries, a drug in the US
Technical and R&D

Bemotrizinol has been marketed in Europe and elsewhere for decades. The FDA order sets use conditions: up to 6%, listed combination limits, propellant-free sprays only.

Regulatory

The US treats sunscreen actives as OTC drugs that need drug-style evidence, so a filter available abroad took years to be added; many other countries treat them as cosmetics and adopt new filters faster. Bemotrizinol was added on 10 June 2026 through the CARES Act administrative-order route, the first success of that route.

Drug Topics Spencer Fane Venable
CMR substances: tolerated in some medicines, prohibited in EU cosmetics
Technical and R&D

Background: a medicine can accept a carcinogenic or reproductive-toxicity risk when a patient gains a therapeutic benefit that outweighs it. A cosmetic is used by healthy people, often daily, and offers no treatment benefit to set against that risk.

Regulatory

Under the EU Cosmetics Regulation, a substance classified CMR 1A, 1B or 2 under CLP is prohibited in cosmetics unless specific exemptions apply. That is why annual "Omnibus" acts copy new CLP classifications into Annex II (18 substances from 1 May 2026; more proposed for 1 Feb 2027).
